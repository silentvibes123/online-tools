In 2026, data breaches and account hacking are more common than ever. The most common reason accounts get hacked is weak or reused passwords. Using a strong, unique password for every account is the single most effective way to protect yourself online.

What Makes a Password Weak?

Common weak passwords people use:
123456
password
india123
yourname@123
mobile number
DOB as password

These passwords can be cracked in under 1 second by modern hacking tools.

What Makes a Password Strong?

A strong password has:
  • At least 12 characters (16+ is better)
  • Mix of uppercase and lowercase letters
  • Numbers (0-9)
  • Special characters (!@#$%^&*)
  • No dictionary words or personal information
  • Unique — not used on any other account

Password Security Tips for Indians

  • Never use your Aadhaar number, PAN number, or mobile number as a password
  • Use different passwords for banking apps, email, and social media
  • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on all important accounts
  • Never share your password on WhatsApp or SMS — banks never ask for it
  • Change passwords immediately if you receive a suspicious login alert
  • Use a password manager instead of writing passwords in a notebook

India-Specific Password & Account Security Tips

Never use these as passwords in India:
  • Your Aadhaar number or last 4 digits of Aadhaar
  • Your PAN card number
  • Your mobile number (especially linked to UPI)
  • Your date of birth in any format (e.g., 01011995)
  • Your UPI PIN — never share this with anyone, ever
Common Indian Scams That Steal Passwords:
  • KYC Update Scam: Fake message saying "Your bank account will be blocked, click link to update KYC" — leads to a fake login page that steals your password
  • OTP Scam: Caller says "I'm from bank, please share the OTP sent to you" — never share OTP with anyone
  • UPI Fraud: "Accept this payment request" — in UPI, you send money when you enter PIN. Accepting a request can debit your account
  • Fake App Scam: Fake "SBI Yono" or "HDFC" apps on WhatsApp — only install banking apps from official app stores

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the generated password stored anywhere?

No. Passwords are generated entirely in your browser using JavaScript's cryptographic random functions. Nothing is sent to any server. We never see or store your passwords.

How often should I change my passwords?

Security experts now recommend changing passwords only when there is a reason — like a data breach or suspicious activity — rather than on a fixed schedule. Using a strong unique password is more important than frequent changes.

What is the best free password manager in India?

Google Password Manager (built into Chrome and Android) is free and works well for most users. Bitwarden is a good open-source option. Both are safer than writing passwords down or reusing them.
